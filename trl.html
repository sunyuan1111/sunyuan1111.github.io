Tactile Grasp Stability / Under Review

QGMM

A large-scale tactile dataset and joint-posture-conditioned grasp-margin representation for dexterous grasp stability prediction.

Question

A visually plausible dexterous grasp can still fail after closure when its load is concentrated, support is unstable, or joint reserve is limited. This work asks how post-contact tactile evidence can predict whether a grasp will withstand external disturbance.

Dataset scale

The dataset contains one million simulated DexHand samples across 100 objects, with 10,000 candidate grasps per object. Each record includes hand joint posture, taxel-level force and contact position, and an ordinal stability label from perturbation trials.

Method pipeline

01Contact executionExecute candidate grasps in simulation and record post-contact per-finger, taxel-level force and object-centered contact positions.
02Perturbation labelsApply multi-directional external forces at multiple levels to obtain an ordinal stability degree rather than label by pose or force closure alone.
03QGMMOrganize the high-dimensional contact field into an 8D descriptor per finger: resultant force, object support point, posture-conditioned weight, and load scale.
04QGMM-liteWhen hardware provides only per-finger resultant forces, estimate support points from known fingertip geometry to retain a deployable low-dimensional representation.

Reported evaluation

01Object-disjointOn the ExtraTrees benchmark, full QGMM raises AP from 0.364 to 0.384.
02QGMM-liteThe aggregate-force branch raises AP from 0.349 to 0.384 against its matched baseline.
03Cross-handAcross AllegroHand and ShadowHand tests, ExtraTrees AP gains range from 0.010 to 0.023.
04Physical transferIn zero-shot physical evaluation with normal-force reconstruction, QGMM-lite raises AP from 0.677 to 0.721.

Research role

This work treats contact reliability after grasp generation as a separate decision: a generator proposes poses, then a stability predictor ranks them from tactile support layout. Beyond object-disjoint prediction, the manuscript reports candidate-pool ranking, design ablations, cross-hand validation, and a 420-execution physical aggregate-force evaluation.
